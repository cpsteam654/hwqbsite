Are rates affordable?

How this is measured

The figure divides the annual residential cost of water and sewer ($1,451) by Jefferson County's median household income ($89,263). This follows the Residential Financial Impact Indicator in the EPA's 2024 Financial Capability Assessment Guidance (Section II.a.1, Exhibit 1), adapted for local benchmarking.

The poverty measure is the EPA's Lowest Quintile Poverty Indicator.

Sustainability

Do rates cover the cost of service?

A utility is on solid footing when what customers pay covers what it costs to serve them. The small amount left over is called the operating margin.

We keep your costs low by keeping our expenses low

03
Protecting customers

Are existing customers protected as the county grows?

Jefferson County is growing, and new homes need new pipes and plant capacity. CTUB collects fees and contributions from new development so that existing customers don't carry that cost alone.

$1+54¢

For every $1 customers pay, CTUB brings in about 54 cents more from growth, grants and interest.

About this comparison

Utilities are usually compared at 3,000 gallons a month using posted rates. That is why CTUB's figure here ($39.00) differs from its $59.98 average water bill, which includes higher-use households.

At the 30,000-gallon commercial level, the private rate is about 1.65 times CTUB's. Sewer is left out: the private utility's Jefferson County wastewater operation is one 125,000-gallon-a-day plant serving roughly 181 customers, too small for a fair comparison.
